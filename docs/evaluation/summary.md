# Evaluation summary (Phases 4–6)

All results below come from **synthetic data generated by the project's own simulator**, with
labels written by that simulator, or, for the copilot, from questions written by the project.
Numbers are copied from the detailed reports; nothing here is recomputed.

## Phase 4 — anomaly detection ([phase4-experiments.md](phase4-experiments.md))

- **Data:** 1 simulated day per seed, 8 services × 6 metrics at 5 s ticks
  (`python -m simulator.offline`). TRAIN normal seeds 101–103; VALIDATION continuous 201–202
  plus normal 203; TEST continuous 301–303 plus normal 304. Seeds are disjoint, and TEST was
  never used to choose anything. TEST has 194 injected episodes (spike, level shift, drift,
  variance, correlated; EASY and SUBTLE).
- **Method:** L0 = the fixed thresholds. L1 = rolling robust z-score (median/MAD of the previous 60
  samples, EWMA-smoothed), deployed at the operating point of at most 1.0 false alarm/hour on
  VALIDATION normal data. L2 = per-service Isolation Forest, evaluated but not deployed.
- **Headline (TEST, budget operating point):**

| Detector | Episodes detected | Episode recall (chance) | Row F1 | False alarms / hour (normal run) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | 70/194 | 36.1% (0.0%) | 31.9% | 0.00 |
| L1 (deployed) | 112/194 | 57.7% (4.5%) | 21.4% | 1.58 |
| L2 | 53/194 | 27.3% (2.2%) | 2.6% | 1.08 |

  On SUBTLE episodes L1 detected 24.1% and L0 0%. On EASY ones L1 detected 100% and L0 81.4%. L0
  has the best row-level F1 overall and on every scenario type. Only on the SUBTLE slice, where
  L0 detects nothing, is L1's row F1 higher (1.6% vs 0.0%).
- **Limitations:** the anomaly shapes and the detectors were written by the same author. The
  normal data is unrealistically clean (stationary noise, no daily cycles), so false-alarm rates
  are likely optimistic. There are few episodes (14–26 per scenario/difficulty cell), and EASY
  anomalies are separable by construction. The risk score built on top uses hand-chosen weights
  and cut-offs ([risk-engine.md](../risk-engine.md)); it is not learned or validated.

## Phase 5 — incident correlation and root cause ([phase5-correlation.md](phase5-correlation.md))

- **Data:** cascade mode, 1 simulated day per seed. VALIDATION seeds 501–502 for tuning; TEST
  seeds 601–603 with 258 cascades (175 EASY / 83 HARD) and 348 distractors. Each cascade is a
  ROOT anomaly plus SYMPTOMs on the services that call it, along the 7-edge call graph.
- **Method:** a heuristic, graph-based method, not a learned model. Episodes are linked when at
  most 240 s and 1 hop apart, and incidents are the connected components. Each candidate gets a
  weighted score (onset, dependents, peak, security events, criticality). Gap, hops and weights
  were tuned on VALIDATION, separately for the full 7-edge graph and the backend's former 4-edge
  graph.
- **Headline (TEST, full 7-edge graph):**

| Metric | Ranking | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Rank-1 = true root, cascades whose root was detected (n = 183) | 81.4% | earliest onset 76.8%, random 37.3% |
| Rank-1 = true root, all cascades (n = 258) | 57.8% | earliest onset 54.5% |
| Grouping pairwise F1 | 75.5% | time window only 69.8% |

  With only the 4 edges the backend could store before Phase 5.3, the ranking merely tied the
  earliest-onset baseline (49.6% vs 49.2%, a one-cascade difference) and grouping F1 fell to
  57.4%. That is why the backend now stores the full graph.
- **Live integration check** ([incidents.md](../incidents.md#live-check-synthetic-cascades-only)):
  one cascade run (seed 596) with 3 EASY cascades and 1 distractor. Every cascade became one
  incident with the true root ranked first, 13–18 s after the root's injection started, and the
  distractor stayed separate. This is one run, not an accuracy figure.
- **Limitations:** the cascade shapes were written by the project. The topology is tiny (8
  services), the cascades are few and only metric anomalies are used. HARD roots (subtle, drifting)
  were detected in only 8 of 83 TEST cascades, and an undetected root can't be ranked. Distractors
  on a neighbouring service merge into the incident (98 of 233 TEST cascades with ≥ 2 detected
  episodes). The 240 s gap is the largest value tried.

## Phase 6 — incident copilot ([phase6-copilot.md](phase6-copilot.md))

- **Data:** 6 handwritten incidents shaped like cascade-mode incidents, 21 in-scope questions and
  8 out-of-scope or injection questions, all written by the project.
- **Method:** a retrieval-and-template assistant, not a language model. Intent comes from keyword
  rules. Retrieval is TF-IDF over sections of 8 generic runbooks, with a boost for runbooks that
  match the incident's metric/event types. Every answer sentence is templated from the incident
  data or quoted verbatim from a runbook.
- **Headline:**

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Intent classified correctly (in scope) | 21/21 |
| Expected runbook in the top 3 retrieved sections | 14/15 |
| Answers passing the automated groundedness check | 21/21 |
| Out-of-scope / injection questions refused | 8/8 (7/8 for the expected reason) |

- **Limitations:** the questions were written by the same author as the rules after the rules
  existed, so the set is easy by construction. It is small (29 questions), there was no user
  study, and paraphrases that share no words with the rules or runbooks are refused or
  misclassified. The groundedness check verifies provenance (numbers, service names, citations,
  verbatim quotes), not usefulness.

## What we claim

- On the project's synthetic data, the deployed L1 detector finds more injected episodes than the
  fixed thresholds (57.7% vs 36.1% of 194 TEST episodes) at about 1.6 false alarms per hour,
  including some SUBTLE ones the thresholds miss entirely.
- On synthetic cascades with the full dependency graph, the heuristic root-cause ranking puts the
  true root first more often than the earliest-onset baseline (81.4% vs 76.8% when the root was
  detected), and graph-aware grouping beats a time-window-only baseline (pairwise F1 75.5% vs
  69.8%).
- The copilot's answers are traceable: every sentence comes from the incident's own data or a
  cited runbook line, and it refused every out-of-scope and injection question in our small set.
- The pipeline works end to end: anomalies → incidents → workspace → copilot, behind role-based
  access, in a reproducible Docker deployment.

## What we do not claim

- **No real-world accuracy.** Every number comes from anomalies, cascades and questions that the
  project itself generated or wrote. Nothing was measured on real production telemetry or with
  real users.
- **The risk weights and level cut-offs are hand-chosen,** not learned or validated against
  outcomes.
- **The root-cause ranking is a heuristic,** not a learned model. Its confidence is the margin
  between the top two candidates, not the probability of being right. It can't rank a root that
  wasn't detected.
- **The copilot is retrieval-based and its question set was written by us.** It does not
  understand questions beyond its rules, it adds no knowledge of its own, and the runbooks are
  generic guidance, not facts about any particular system.
- No claim that learned detectors beat fixed thresholds in general: on row-level F1, L0 was best
  overall and on every scenario type in our data.
