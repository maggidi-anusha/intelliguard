# Phase 5.2 — incident correlation and root-cause evaluation

> **Scope:** measured on **synthetic cascades injected by the project's own simulator**, with
> labels written by the simulator. Not real-world accuracy. The correlation and root-cause method
> is a **heuristic, graph-based ranking — not a learned model**.

Generated by `ml-service/evaluation/run_correlation_experiments.py` (2026-10-10 03:13 UTC, 32 s). Reproduce from `ml-service/`:

```
python evaluation/run_correlation_experiments.py --val-seeds 501 502 --test-seeds 601 602 603 \
    --train-seeds 101 102 103 --days 1
```

## Setup

- **Data:** `python -m simulator.offline --mode cascade`, 1 simulated day(s) per seed, 8 services × 6 metrics, 5 s ticks.
  A cascade is a ROOT anomaly plus SYMPTOM anomalies on the services that call it (transitively,
  along the 7-edge call graph), each starting 10–60 s after the service it calls. EASY cascades
  have a strong root and weaker symptoms; HARD cascades have a subtle, slowly drifting root and
  clearly visible symptoms. 0–2 independent DISTRACTOR anomalies on unrelated services overlap
  each cascade in time.
- **Detector:** the deployed L1 (window 60, alpha 0.2, threshold 2.1532),
  MAD floors fitted on TRAIN normal-mode seeds 101, 102, 103.
- **Episodes:** per (service, metric), opened on the first flagged 5 s tick, extended while flagged,
  closed after 6 unflagged ticks (30 s ≈ the backend's 3 unflagged 10 s cycles); start/end =
  first/last flagged tick; peakScore = max L1 score. A detected episode belongs to a ground-truth
  label when service and metric match and it overlaps the label window (+60 s);
  otherwise it is a detector false alarm ("noise").
- **Correlation:** `app/correlation.py`, the same code as `POST /correlate`. The security events
  passed to the ranking are those in each incident's window (start − 60 s to end).
- **Graphs:** full call graph (7 edges) vs the 4 edges the backend's single `dependsOnServiceId`
  can store (missing api-gateway→auth-service, api-gateway→order-service, order-service→inventory-service).
- **Tuning (VALIDATION only, separately per graph):** gap ∈ [30, 60, 120, 240] s and max_hops ∈ [1, 2, 3]
  by pairwise F1 (with fixed default weights), then the weights from 8 candidate
  vectors by top-1 accuracy over all cascades. The time-only baseline's gap is tuned the same way.

## Findings

- **full 7-edge graph:** the weighted ranking beats the earliest-onset baseline on TEST top-1 over all cascades (57.8% vs 54.5%; 81.4% vs 76.8% where the root was detected).
- **backend 4-edge graph:** the weighted ranking **is effectively tied with** (a difference of 1 cascade) the earliest-onset baseline on TEST top-1 over all cascades (49.6% vs 49.2%; 69.9% vs 69.4% where the root was detected).
- **HARD cascades are mostly unsolved:** top-1 is 2.4% with the full graph, because L1 detected the subtle root in only 8 of 83 HARD cascades; when the root is never detected, no ranking of the detected services can be right.
- **Grouping errors differ by graph:** with the full graph 98 of 233 multi-episode cascades absorbed a distractor (almost every service is within one hop of another in this 8-service topology); with the backend's 4 edges 149 of 233 were split (the missing edges disconnect api-gateway and inventory-service); time alone merged 149 with a distractor.
- **Grouping:** pairwise F1 75.5% with the full graph, 57.4% with the backend's 4 edges, 69.8% with time alone - so with only the backend's 4 edges, adding the graph makes grouping worse than ignoring it.
- **Cost of the single-dependency limitation:** top-1 over all cascades is 8.1 points lower with the backend's 4 edges than with the full graph; pairwise F1 differs by +18.0 points (full − backend).

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data only, cascade shapes authored by us.** The propagation rule (callers, 10–60 s
  delay per hop, weaker symptoms) was written by the same people who designed the ranking, so the
  ranking's onset and dependents inputs match the generator's assumptions by construction.
- **Tiny topology.** 8 services and 7 edges; almost every pair is within 2–3 hops via api-gateway,
  which limits how much the graph can separate unrelated anomalies.
- **Small number of cascades** (about 258 on TEST); per-type cells hold a few dozen cascades.
- **Metric anomalies only** — no traces or logs are correlated; security events only enter the ranking.
- **Detector-dependent.** Results include L1's misses and false alarms; subtle (HARD) roots are often not detected at all.
- **One dependency per service in the backend.** The 4-edge results show what that costs today.
- **Coarse tuning grid.** The best gap for both graphs (240 s) is the largest value in the grid; a
  wider grid might do better. It was not extended, to avoid changing the method after seeing TEST.

## Anything changed after seeing TEST

No parameter, grid, objective or episode rule was changed after the TEST run. After seeing TEST, only the wording of the findings was revised: differences of 2 cascades or fewer are now described as ties (the first draft called a 1-cascade difference a win), and the HARD-cascade and grouping-outcome findings plus the grid-edge limitation were added. Re-running produced identical numbers.
